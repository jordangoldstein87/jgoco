Where locations come from

Your own location historyConnected

Email, location agreements, scout photos, call sheets. Every place you’ve scouted or shot, with fees, contacts, power and parking, pulled out of what you already have.

Google MapsAPI

Places, Street View, drive times. Finds the nearest ER for a new set, drive time from basecamp, parking and a street-level look before anyone scouts.

Weather, tide and sunAPI

National Weather Service, NOAA tides, sunrise and sunset. Free public data. Fills the call sheet’s forecast, tide table and sunset on its own.

Peerspace and GiggsterBrowser search

Rentable homes, studios and venues. No public API, so the assistant searches them in your browser, signed in as you. Booking emails then file the location, dates and fee to the show.

Film commission librariesBrowser search

Location galleries run on Reel-Scout and similar. The assistant searches the public galleries for you, and packages a commission sends by link land on the show.

FilmLA and local permit officesTracked from email

Permit applications and status. No public API. Permit confirmations and riders are read from email and put on the calendar and the location’s file.

Email does the filing

Each person gets a schedule that builds itself as bookings, fittings and call times come in.

How it works

Connect once. It files everything after.

The line producer and each PM connect their own email. Anyone else at the company can too, along with Slack or Teams, their calendar, and Google Meet, so call notes come in ready to add to a show. It reads what arrives, works out which show it belongs to, and suggests what to do with it. Setting it up is the big lift. After that it learns from how your people work.
